TALK TITLE: Core Knowledge Systems: Objects, Places, Agents, and Number
SPEAKER: Prof. Liz Spelke (Harvard University)
DATE: Friday, December 4 & Saturday, December 5, 2026 • 8:00 AM IST (Two-Day Morning Colloquium)

ABSTRACT:
How does human knowledge arise? In this two-part morning series, I present evidence that human cognition is built upon phylogenetic foundations of 'core knowledge'—specialized cognitive systems that emerge early in ontogeny, operate across the lifespan, and are shared with other non-human animals.

Part I (Dec 4) focuses on the core systems of inanimate objects and navigational geometry. We examine how infants represent cohesive, bounded bodies that move continuously through space, and how spatial layouts guide reorientation. Part II (Dec 5) examines social cognition: the representation of goal-directed agents, cooperative partners, and early numerical systems. We close by exploring how distinct core knowledge systems are uniquely unified in humans through language and symbolic education.
