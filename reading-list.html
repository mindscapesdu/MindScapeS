Reading List

Suggested preparation before each talk.

To encourage informed discussion during the Student-First Q&A and open debate, speakers provide one or two recommended papers prior to their session. Reading these papers is encouraged but not required for attendance.

Prof. Melissa Kibbe (October 30, 2026)

Kibbe, M. M., & Leslie, A. M. (2019). "Conceptually rich, perceptually sparse: Object representations in infancy." Cognitive Psychology, 110, 42–61. [DOI / Link]
Why it is relevant: Establishes how infants maintain distinct representations of object identity versus surface feature bindings in working memory.
Kibbe, M. M. (2015). "Varieties of visual working memory representation in infancy and beyond." Current Directions in Psychological Science, 24(6), 433–439. [DOI / Link]
Why it is relevant: Provides a concise conceptual overview of the cognitive architecture underlying early numerical and spatial tracking.

Prof. Susanna Schellenberg (November 6, 2026)

Schellenberg, S. (2018). The Unity of Perception: Content, Consciousness, Evidence. Oxford University Press. (Chapter 2: "Perceptual Capacities"). [Publisher Link]
Why it is relevant: Introduces the capacitive framework of perception, arguing that perceptual experience is grounded in discriminative capacities.
Schellenberg, S. (2016). "Perceptual consciousness as a mental capacity." Noûs, 50(1), 145–171. [DOI / Link]
Why it is relevant: Clarifies how phenomenal character connects directly to epistemic justification and environmental grounding.

Prof. Edward Gibson (November 13, 2026)

Gibson, E., Futrell, R., Piantadosi, S. P., Dautriche, I., Mahowald, K., Bergen, L., & Levy, R. (2019). "How efficiency shapes human language." Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 23(5), 389–407. [DOI / Link]
Why it is relevant: Synthesizes cross-linguistic evidence showing that grammatical structures and lexicons are optimized for communicative efficiency under processing bounds.

Access note: When open-access versions are available via institutional repositories (ArXiv, PsyArXiv, or university libraries), links are provided directly above. Contact organizers if you have difficulty accessing preparatory readings.
